An honest comparison

App, language school, marketplace or private lessons?

Four ways to learn Italian, with their real strengths and weaknesses — including the cases where you don’t need me at all.

This is the page where I explain why you might work with me. I’ve tried to be honest about it, because the alternative — claiming to be best at everything — convinces nobody anyway.

An app like Duolingo

Apps are better than their reputation. They keep you going, they’re free or cheap, and for building basic vocabulary they’re excellent. Anyone claiming you learn nothing from them has never used one seriously.

Where they fall down is production. An app checks whether you tapped the right answer. It doesn’t hear you swallowing the double consonant, doesn’t notice that you avoid the congiuntivo by rebuilding your sentences, and doesn’t correct the mistake you’ve been repeating for six months without knowing. That’s why so many people stall at the same point after a long streak: they understand a lot and say very little.

Use an app if you’re starting out, want vocabulary, or need something between lessons.
Don’t rely on an app if you’ve been at it for months and speaking isn’t keeping up.

A group course at a language school

Good language schools do good work, and a group offers things a private lesson can’t: classmates, a fixed structure, a lower hourly price, and the social pressure that gets you out of the house on a dark Tuesday in November.

The two limits are structural, not a matter of quality. The first is speaking time: in a group of twelve, with a teacher who also has to explain things, you get a few minutes per lesson. The second is pace: it’s the group’s average pace, which is almost never exactly yours. On top of that, courses start on fixed dates — usually September and February — and the offer often stops around B2.

Take a group course if you’re a beginner, you want the social side, budget matters, and September suits you anyway.
Don’t if you have a deadline, want to go past B2, or work irregular hours.

A marketplace like italki or Preply

Wide choice, low barrier to entry, and you’ll find genuinely good teachers there — I know, because I work on those platforms myself. If you want pure conversation hours at a sharp price, this is a fine choice.

The weak point is continuity. You book single hours, often with rotating teachers, usually with no ongoing programme. The hour is pleasant and there is no plan. One of my students put it like this:

“Unlike many Italki teachers, whose classes often lack structure, Alessio is very well-organized: he prepares each class carefully and always adapts it to my own interests.”

— Matthijs

Use a marketplace if you only want speaking hours, price is decisive, or you want to try several teachers.
Don’t if you want a built-up path with someone who remembers where you got stuck last month.

What I do differently

There are two things you don’t get from the other three. Neither of them is “personal attention” — everyone says that.

1. I’m trained in teaching Italian, not just in Italian

I’m a native speaker, but that isn’t the point: there are plenty of us. I hold two master’s degrees specifically in teaching Italian to speakers of other languages — Università Ca’ Foscari Venezia (ITALS) and Università Giustino Fortunato.

In practice that means I know why Dutch and English speakers trip over the congiuntivo and not over word order, why the double consonant is nearly inaudible to a Germanic ear, and in what order to introduce things so they stick. That’s a discipline, and it’s a different one from speaking the language.

2. I write my own material

Almost every language school and every platform works from a commercial coursebook, written for an average learner who doesn’t exist. I don’t use a coursebook. What you get I made myself, and it stays yours:

  • Over 700 interactive exercises — free, no account, from A1 to B2.
  • My own iOS app with a complete CEFR path from A1 to C2 and a dictionary of more than 4,000 words in fourteen languages.
  • Italian classics rewritten by level — with notes and a glossary, so you’re reading Pirandello at B1.

If you’re going to Puglia in May, I write the lesson about Puglia. If you need to understand an Italian quote, we work on that quote. A coursebook cannot do that by definition.

When you don’t need me

If you just want a few phrases for your holiday: get an app. If you mainly want company and a low hourly rate: take a group course in your town. If you want pure speaking hours with no programme: a marketplace is cheaper than me.

And if you’d rather carry on alone: the 700 exercises and the app are there whether or not you ever book a lesson. That’s not a hook — it’s genuinely there, and it’s free.

When I am the right choice

  • You’ve stalled at B1 or B2: you understand a lot, but speaking isn’t keeping up.
  • You want to go beyond B2, and the offer in your area stops there.
  • You have a real deadline: a move, a certificate, a client in Milan.
  • You work irregular hours and don’t fit a fixed timetable.
  • You want one teacher who remembers your path instead of isolated hours.
