Modi Di Dire

Cascare dal pero

15 July 2026

by Alessio · teacher of Italian as a foreign language, ITALS master's

What does “cascare dal pero” mean? Literally, “to fall out of the pear tree”: to be — or pretend to be — completely astonished by something that was obvious or well known to everyone else. English gets close with “to act as if you’d fallen from the moon” or the dry “don’t act so surprised”.

You will mostly hear it with a hint of reproach: ma da che pero sei cascato? — how could you possibly not know this? It also covers the moment someone finally realises a truth the whole room had already accepted. Informal register, everyday conversation.

The image is rural Italy at its best: someone daydreaming up in a pear tree who suddenly crashes back down to earth — dazed, blinking, out of touch with what everyone below already knew.

Examples

Quando gli ho detto che il progetto era annullato, è cascato dal pero. — When I told him the project had been cancelled, he acted completely stunned.

Non cascare dal pero: lo sapevano tutti da settimane. — Don’t act so surprised: everyone had known for weeks.

Solo ieri è cascata dal pero e ha capito perché nessuno la chiamava. — Only yesterday did it finally dawn on her why nobody was calling.

Similar expressions in Italian

Cadere dalle nuvole (to fall from the clouds — the most common synonym), rimanere di stucco (to be dumbfounded).

Exercise

1. Your colleague “discovers” a rule that has been in the handbook for years. Can you say “sei cascato dal pero”?

Show answer

Yes: that is exactly the point — astonishment about something long established.

2. A friend is genuinely surprised by breaking news nobody could have known. “Cascare dal pero”?

Show answer

No: the idiom implies the information was already common knowledge; fresh news does not qualify.
