The Test Says A2: What Do I Do Now

5 August 2026

The test says A2. The normal reaction is a small disappointment, followed by a resolution to start again from scratch. Both are wrong, and the second one costs months.

What A2 actually means

It means you cope with every predictable situation: shopping, making an appointment, saying how your day went, ordering at a restaurant and understanding the answer. You use the passato prossimo, the future, the first connectives. That is not nothing: it is the level where Italian stops being a subject and starts being a language you use.

What is missing is the unexpected. As long as the conversation stays on the track you know, everything works. The moment it leaves the track, you stop. B1 is exactly that: holding on when it leaves the track.

The first thing not to do

Start again from A1. It is almost everyone's instinct and almost always a waste. If your test measured the four skills separately, look at them: two are very likely close to B1 already and one is far behind. Redoing everything means spending most of your time on what you already know, while the gap that is blocking you stays where it is.

The rule: your level does not rise from doing more exercises in general. It rises when you close the widest distance between the four measures. If you do not know which one that is, that is the first problem to solve.

Three cases, and what to do in each

Grammar ahead, vocabulary behind. The classroom learner's case. What you need is reading, not studying. Texts at your level, twenty minutes a day, without a dictionary every second word. Graded readers by level exist for exactly this: they are written to put new words in front of you at a density that does not stop you.

Vocabulary ahead, grammar behind. The case of people who learned by living in Italy. Here the work is on a few high-yield structures: imperfetto against passato prossimo, combined pronouns, the subjunctive in the two or three sentence types where it genuinely matters. Not all of grammar: those.

Everything average, production stuck. The case of people who studied alone with apps. You understand, you recognise, you pick the right option, and then nothing comes out in front of a blank page. You need to produce: four written lines a day, and somebody to correct them.

A three-month plan that works

Twenty minutes of reading a day aimed at the weakest point. Four written lines, every day, however dull. One weekly review of what you wrote, done by somebody who knows where to look. Nothing else.

It looks like very little because it is: the reason it works is not the quantity, it is that it hits one point instead of spreading across four.

When to retake the test

In three months, not sooner. Under three months the differences sit inside the margin of error of any test, and retaking it fortnightly produces nothing but frustration. If you want to see progress meanwhile, keep the four lines you write each day: rereading the ones from twelve weeks ago is the most honest measure there is.
