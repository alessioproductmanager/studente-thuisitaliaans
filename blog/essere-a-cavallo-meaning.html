Modi Di Dire

Essere a cavallo

20 July 2026

by Alessio · teacher of Italian as a foreign language, ITALS master's

What does “essere a cavallo” mean? Literally, “to be on horseback”. Once you are in the saddle, the hardest part is behind you and the road ahead is easy: you are home and dry, over the hump. Italians say it the moment an obstacle falls and the rest looks like a formality: firmato il contratto, siamo a cavallo!

The image comes from a world where the horse was the difference between walking for days and travelling in comfort: whoever had one was, quite simply, in a winning position. That is the exact nuance today — not “finished”, but “the worst is over and victory is close”.

Register: informal, very common in speech. You will often hear the short burst siamo a cavallo! as a small cry of relief.

Examples

Superato l’esame di teoria, sei a cavallo: la pratica è la parte facile. — Once the theory exam is done, you’re home and dry: the practical is the easy part.

Trovata la casa, siamo a cavallo. — Now that we’ve found the flat, the rest is a formality.

Con questo cliente firmato, l’azienda è a cavallo per tutto l’anno. — With this client signed, the company is set for the whole year.

Similar expressions in Italian

Essere in una botte di ferro (“in an iron barrel” — completely safe), avere la strada spianata (to have the road smoothed out ahead).

Exercise

1. You have passed the hardest exam of your degree; two easy ones remain. What can you say?

Show answer

“Sono a cavallo!” — the biggest obstacle is behind you, the rest looks manageable.

2. You are halfway through a difficult negotiation with everything still open. “Siamo a cavallo”?

Show answer

Not yet: the idiom needs the decisive obstacle to be already cleared. Halfway through, it would sound premature.
