Learn Italian in Chunks: Phrases Beat Word Lists

3 June 2026

Nobody assembles in bocca al lupo word by word, and no grammar rule produces ci vediamo, ti va di...? or non vedo l'ora. These are chunks: prefabricated stretches of language stored and retrieved whole. Corpus research keeps finding that a very large share of fluent native speech is built from such ready-made material (Alison Wray's work on formulaic language is the standard reference), and this is excellent news for learners, because chunks can simply be learned, the way words are.

Why chunks buy fluency and accuracy at once

Fluency first: retrieving non vedo l'ora di vederti as one stored unit costs a fraction of the processing of building it from seven words and three grammar decisions. Native speed is largely this: less computing, more retrieving. A learner with two hundred solid chunks sounds dramatically more fluent than one with two thousand loose words, because the chunks come out at speech speed. This is the same economy that fluency practice trains from another angle.

Accuracy second, and this is the quiet gift: chunks carry their grammar inside. Learn ti va di + infinitive as a block and the preposition problem never arises, because the right preposition is welded in. Half the classic preposition and article errors in Italian dissolve when the unit of learning is the phrase rather than the word, which also answers a puzzle from the vocabulary size article: knowing a word "with the right preposition attached" is really knowing its chunks.

Where to harvest them: dialogues

Chunks live in interaction. Narrative prose teaches you narrative language; it is dialogue that teaches meno male, figurati, dai, che ne so, the connective tissue of actual conversation. So the harvesting method is simple: work with real dialogues, and mine them deliberately.

  • Collect by function, not alphabet: keep a page per situation: agreeing, refusing politely, buying time (allora... dunque... come dire...). Filler chunks are legitimate vocabulary; natives run on them.
  • Learn the chunk with its slot: note ti va di + infinito, not just the frozen string, so you can reuse the frame: ti va di uscire? di mangiare qualcosa?
  • Drill them as wholes: chunks go into your spaced review as complete phrases, tested by production: say the whole thing, at speed, out loud.
  • Shadow dialogue audio: shadowing welds the chunk to its intonation, which is half of what makes it sound native.

Dialogues as a course backbone

This research is why dialogues in ti are core material rather than decoration (the supply across the levels is practically endless, with new ones arriving all the time), and why its conversation exercise "Botta e risposta" works the way it does: a real dialogue stops mid-flow and you choose the line that coherently continues it, a test of whether you command the pragmatic chunks a situation demands, not just its grammar (more in the output article). The themed Edicola books do the situational harvesting for you: bar Italian, work Italian, football Italian. Each one is effectively a chunk collection organised by the situations of real life.

The mindset shift is the takeaway: stop asking only "what does this word mean?" and start asking "what does this whole phrase do?" Italian is not a bag of words with rules; a large part of it is a repertoire of moves. Learn the moves.
