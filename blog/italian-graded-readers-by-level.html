Italian graded readers: which classic at which level

26 July 2026

The usual first attempt goes like this. You buy an Italian novel, you read two pages with a dictionary next to you, it takes forty minutes, and you put it down. Not because you are not good enough, but because the book was not written for where you are.

A graded reader is not a lesser book. It is the same story with the vocabulary and sentence structure adjusted to a level. The objection is always that it is not the real book, and that is true. But the alternative is not reading the real book. The alternative is not reading it at all.

The rule for picking a level

Open any page and count the words you do not know.

  • 0 to 2 per page: too easy. Pleasant, but you are not learning much. Go up.
  • 3 to 8 per page: right. You follow the story and pick up words along the way.
  • more than 15: too hard. You are decoding, not reading.

The temptation is to aim high, because the level printed on the cover feels like a judgement about you. It is not. A B1 reader who finishes a B1 book learns more than a B1 reader who abandons a B2 one.

A1: start earlier than you think

Cuore by Edmondo De Amicis. A school year in Turin told by a boy, in short chapters that stand on their own. That last part matters at A1: you do not have to hold a hundred pages of plot in your head.

Also at this level: Gian Burrasca, the diary of a boy who causes trouble on every page. Funny, which helps more than people admit.

A2: when the past tense arrives

Pinocchio by Carlo Collodi. You already know the story, so your attention goes to the language instead of the plot. And it is darker and stranger than the Disney version, so it stays interesting.

Also here: Il Corsaro Nero by Salgari for adventure, and the Lettere di Viaggio of Columbus and Vespucci if you prefer something historical.

B1: the turning point

This is where you read a story for the story.

Canne al Vento by Grazia Deledda. Sardinia, a family in decline, written by the only Italian woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. The atmosphere is strong and the sentences are short.

For something more playful: Il Fu Mattia Pascal by Pirandello, about a man who reads his own death in the newspaper and decides to start again.

B2: the big names

I Promessi Sposi by Manzoni. The Italian novel, the one every Italian reads at school and everyone quotes. In the original it is heavy going. Adapted at B2 it is entirely manageable, and it unlocks a hundred references you were missing.

Also at B2: Il Milione by Marco Polo, and Sidereus Nuncius by Galileo if you like the history of science.

C1: literature, barely touched

Il Piacere by D'Annunzio for the style, or I Viceré by De Roberto for a Sicilian family chronicle full of irony. At this level it is no longer about understanding but about noticing how something is written.

How to actually read them

Do not look up every word. If you stop at each unknown word you are translating, not reading, and the whole benefit disappears.

Skip what the story does not need. A tree, an item of clothing, an adjective. If it matters, you will meet it again.

Look up what comes back three times. That is the signal the word is worth having.

Read each chapter twice. First for the story, without stopping. Then again for what you missed. The second pass takes half the time and gives you twice as much.
