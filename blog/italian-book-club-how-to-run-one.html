Running an Italian book club when nobody is fluent yet

26 July 2026

The hardest part of reading in a second language is not the language. It is week three, when the book is on the table and something else is easier. A group solves that, and almost nothing else does.

Reading clubs for Italian learners are not a new idea. Some existing clubs work through a classic over a summer, with a simplified version available for members who cannot manage the original. That model works, and you can run it privately with four friends.

Choosing the book

Pick for the weakest reader, not the strongest. This is the decision that determines whether the club survives. Someone who cannot follow drops out in week two, and clubs do not recover from that.

Choose a book with chapters. Twenty short chapters give you a natural rhythm. A dense novel with fifty page sections does not.

Choose something you can talk about. A story with moral weight generates discussion; a language exercise does not. This is the practical argument for real classics over invented graded-reader plots: Canne al vento gives a group something to argue about, and a story written to practise the past tense does not.

Setting the pace

Two chapters a week, meeting every two weeks. Ten weeks for a twenty chapter book.

Slower than that and people forget the plot between meetings. Faster and the people with jobs fall behind and stop coming. Whatever you choose, protect the date: a club that moves its meeting twice has stopped existing.

The discussion language question

This is where most groups get it wrong in one of two directions.

All in Italian from day one means the strongest speaker talks and everyone else nods. You get a monologue, not a discussion.

All in English means the club is about literature, not language. Perfectly valid, but be honest that that is what it is.

What works is splitting the session. Twenty minutes in Italian on what happened, using the vocabulary from the chapter, then switch to English for what you thought about it. The first half is the exercise; the second is the reason people come back.

Structure for a session

Five minutes on the plot, in Italian, going round the group. Twenty minutes on the passages people marked, which is where the language work actually happens. Then open discussion in whatever language, and five minutes to agree the next reading.

Ask everyone to bring one sentence they liked and one they did not understand. That single rule turns a vague conversation into a session with content.

If you want a teacher in the room

Not necessary, and it changes the thing. A club is peers reading together; the moment someone is being paid, people wait to be told the answer instead of arguing.

What does work is a teacher for one session in four: the group reads on its own, and once a month someone goes through the passages nobody could crack. If that suits your group, get in touch and we can look at how to set it up.
