Are You Ready for the Italian B1 Exam? An Honest Check

2 July 2026

Booking an exam is easy. Knowing whether you should is the real question, and there are three honest ways to answer it before paying the fee. I say honest deliberately, because self assessment in languages drifts optimistic: we remember our best sentences and forget the merged audio we missed. A teacher's job is partly to be the mirror; here is how to be your own.

Check one: a placement test that stops where you stop

A proper placement test climbs through the levels with real material and stops where your errors accumulate, rather than awarding a flattering label after ten easy questions. The ti app runs its entrance placement exactly like that, adaptive from A1 to C2, built from the actual course material, free, and finished in minutes. If it places you at B1 or above, proceed to check three. If it places you at A2, you have not failed anything; you have been given a truthful starting line, and the route from there is the preparation plan.

Check two: the can-do mirror

The CEFR defines B1 as things you can do, and you can hold yourself against those statements directly: I can follow clear speech on familiar matters. I can deal with most situations while travelling. I can write a simple connected text. I can enter a conversation unprepared and survive it. The discipline is to tie every yes to a real memory of doing it, in each of the five skills separately, because profiles are jagged and a B1 reader with A2 listening is the normal case, not a curiosity. The full method, including the portfolio that turns your writing into evidence, is in the can-do article, and the levels themselves are explained in the CEFR guide.

Check three: the only answer that counts

The decisive check is the exam itself, sat at home: a complete B1 mock test, timed, scored section by section against the thresholds. Pass three consecutive full simulations comfortably and you are ready; book with confidence. Fail one section repeatedly and you have found next month's work, which is a bargain compared to finding it in the exam room. This is precisely what the 35 complete mock tests in the ti app's B1 exam section are for: the citizenship exam, rehearsed until the result stops being a question. Why simulations are this predictive is explained in the mock exam article.

Three checks, one afternoon to start them, and the fee stays in your pocket until the evidence says spend it. That is what readiness means. The full context of the citizenship requirement is in the complete guide.
