Italian reflexive verbs

Italians use reflexive verbs constantly — often where English doesn't. Here's how.

Download on the App Store

How they work

A reflexive verb needs its reflexive pronoun before the conjugated form: mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si.

  • lavarsi (to wash oneself): mi lavo, ti lavi, si lava, ci laviamo, vi lavate, si lavano
  • alzarsi (to get up): mi alzo, ti alzi, si alza...
  • chiamarsi (to be called): mi chiamo, ti chiami, si chiama...

In the passato prossimo, always essere: mi sono lavato/a.

Common daily-life reflexives

  • svegliarsi (wake up), alzarsi (get up), lavarsi (wash), vestirsi (get dressed), pettinarsi (comb), truccarsi (put makeup on)
  • arrabbiarsi (get angry), divertirsi (have fun), annoiarsi (get bored), stancarsi (get tired)
  • ricordarsi (remember), dimenticarsi (forget), preoccuparsi (worry), sposarsi (get married)

Reciprocal reflexives

Some reflexives mean "each other": ci vediamo domani (we'll see each other tomorrow), si parlano (they talk to each other), ci scriviamo (we write to each other).
