For people learning Italian

Reading Manzoni in Simple Italian: I promessi sposi at B2

This is the book that decided what modern Italian would sound like. It is also the book that has ended more Italian reading habits than any other.

The novel that invented the language you are learning

Manzoni was a Milanese who wrote in Lombard-inflected Italian, hated the result, moved to Florence and rewrote the whole novel in Florentine. He called it risciacquare i panni in Arno — rinsing the laundry in the Arno. That second version, published in 1840, became the model for standard written Italian.

So when you learn Italian, you are in a real sense learning Manzoni’s choices. That is a good reason to read him. It is not a good reason to read him too early.

What actually happens

Lombardy, 1628. Two peasants, Renzo and Lucia, want to marry. A local lord decides they will not. What follows is a chase across a country falling apart: famine, riots in Milan, a mercenary war, and the plague of 1630 rendered in some of the most exact prose in the language.

It is not a romance. It is a book about power — who has it, what it costs to be without it, and how ordinary people survive the people above them.

Why the original defeats B1 readers

  • Sentence length. Manzoni’s periods run for six or eight lines with three subordinate clauses. By the verb you have forgotten the subject.
  • Nineteenth-century vocabulary. Words that are still in dictionaries and dead in speech.
  • The digressions. Whole chapters of seventeenth-century administrative history. Italians skip them too.
  • Length. Around 700 pages. At two pages an hour you will not finish.

None of this means the book is beyond you. It means the 1840 text is beyond you, which is a different statement.

Reading it at B2

The graded edition keeps the plot, the characters and Manzoni’s irony — which is the best thing in the book and the first thing summaries lose. Sentences come down to a length you can hold in your head; the vocabulary sits at B2; footnotes handle what is left.

Twenty chapters, a glossary at the back, comprehension questions at the end of each one. See I promessi sposi at B2, or the rest of the B2 level.

If B2 is still a stretch, read Canne al vento at B1 first and come back. Manzoni will still be there.
