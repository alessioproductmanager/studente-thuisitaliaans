How to Choose Your Italian Reading Level (A1 to C1)

Almost everyone picks one level too high. It feels like ambition and it works like a wall.

The one-page test

Open any book at a page in the middle — not the first page, which is always the easiest. Read one page normally. Count how many times you stop.

0–1 stops per pageToo easy. Go up a level. You will get bored by chapter three.
2–5 stops per pageRight. This is where reading builds vocabulary without becoming work.
6–10 stops per pageOne level too high. You will finish it and enjoy nothing.
More than 10Two levels too high. You will stop within a week and blame yourself.

“Stop” means you break the flow to look something up or reread. Guessing a word from context and carrying on does not count — that is exactly what you want to be doing.

What each level means in practice

  • A1 — present tense, a few hundred words. Short chapters, concrete scenes, no irony. A1 books
  • A2 — past tenses, everyday vocabulary. Longer stories, some description. A2 books
  • B1 — you follow a conversation between natives. Full novels, subordinate clauses, a narrator with a voice. B1 books
  • B2 — you read a newspaper without effort. Nineteenth-century plots, formal registers, irony. B2 books
  • C1 — you want the original difficulty back. Literary syntax, rare vocabulary, style as content. C1 books

Two rules that are worth more than the test

Read one level below your speaking level. Most learners understand more than they say, but reading is where you build the automaticity that speaking spends. Comfortable input beats hard input.

Never look up a word twice on the same page. If it matters, it will come back. If it does not come back, it did not matter.
