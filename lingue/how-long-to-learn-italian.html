How long does it really take to learn Italian?

Short answer: depending on your goal, anywhere from 3 months (A2, citizenship level) to 3 years (C2, near-native). Long answer below, with the hours per level.

The official numbers

The Council of Europe estimates the following guided-learning hours to reach each CEFR level in a Romance language like Italian, starting from zero:

  • A1 — 90–100 hours
  • A2 — 180–200 hours (total)
  • B1 — 350–400 hours
  • B2 — 500–600 hours
  • C1 — 700–800 hours
  • C2 — 1000–1200 hours

These are conservative — they assume classroom hours. Add roughly 50–100% for self-study, since you don't have a teacher correcting you in real time.

What that means in real months

Assuming you study 30 minutes a day (a realistic daily habit):

  • A1 — around 6 months
  • A2 — around 12 months (this is the citizenship level)
  • B1 — around 20 months
  • B2 — around 30 months

If you can push to 1 hour a day, halve those numbers. If you live in Italy and use Italian daily outside study, halve them again.

What each level feels like

  • A1 — You can survive a coffee order, a hotel check-in, an introduction. You'll misunderstand a lot.
  • A2 — You can handle daily errands and simple work chat. Reading is slow but possible. You pass the citizenship test.
  • B1 — You can hold a real conversation. Watching TV is exhausting but doable with subtitles.
  • B2 — You can work in Italian. You can watch TV without subtitles for most content.
  • C1 — You start noticing register and idioms. Literature becomes readable.
  • C2 — Near-native. You get jokes and dialect references.

How to actually speed it up

  1. Daily short sessions beat weekly long ones. 30 minutes a day trumps 3 hours on Saturday.
  2. Speak from week one. Even bad Italian, out loud. The mouth needs to practise more than the brain.
  3. Consume real Italian input. Podcasts (Coffee Break Italian at A1, News in Slow Italian at A2), YouTube, Italian TV.
  4. Get a conversation partner once you can string sentences (~end of A1). Cheap on italki or free on HelloTalk.
  5. Track a real CEFR path so you know what to study next. Random content = random progress.
