CAPITOLO 1

L’attesa

The Waiting

L'ultimo giorno dell'anno, nel palazzo Zuccari, Andrea Sperelli aspettava una donna. Aveva fatto preparare la stanza come si prepara un altare: le rose in ogni vaso, il fuoco di ginepro nel camino, i tè rari, le tazze antiche, i cuscini disposti con quella squisitezza che gli costava ore e che nessuno avrebbe dovuto notare.1 Ogni oggetto era stato scelto, provato, spostato: il lusso, in lui, non era ricchezza: era una lingua. Guardò l'orologio. Elena Muti sarebbe salita fra poco quelle scale, dopo due anni di silenzio. E lui, che aveva composto quella scena con la cura di un'opera d'arte, si accorse di avere le mani fredde.

squisitezza = exquisiteness · lusso = luxury · arte = art

1 Il romanzo comincia con un uomo che allestisce una scena. È la chiave di tutto il libro: Andrea non vive i propri sentimenti, li mette in scena, e la cura che dedica agli oggetti è la stessa che dedicherà alle persone.

The plot of Il piacere

Rome, splendid and decadent. Count Andrea Sperelli, a refined aesthete, poet and seducer, has made his own life a work of art. But he is torn between two women: Elena Muti, the sensual passion who abandoned him, and Maria Ferres, the spiritual love who might save him.

In the salons, antique auctions and baroque churches of the capital, Andrea pursues pleasure until he loses himself. The manifesto novel of Italian Decadentism, written in prose of dazzling beauty.

Why read Il piacere to learn Italian

The most elegant prose of the Italian nineteenth century, made climbable by footnotes and glossary: the ideal training ground before tackling D'Annunzio's originals. The vocabulary of art and beauty is a treasure for anyone who loves discussing culture in Italian.

Inside the glossary

Glossary

anima · the soul ((sin. spirito, interiorità))
La parte spirituale e interiore dell'uomo.
The spiritual, inner part of a person.
Andrea invokes the soul when it suits him, to ennoble desire. It is perhaps his most refined lie: calling spirit what is only appetite.
aristocrazia · aristocracy ((sin. nobiltà, élite))
La classe dei nobili; per estensione, chi si ritiene superiore per nascita o per gusto.
The class of the nobility; those who deem themselves superior by birth or taste.
Roman aristocracy in 1885 governs nothing anymore: it has only taste left, the last privilege. D'Annunzio paints it beautiful and finished: and knows he belongs to it.
arte · art ((sin. opera, creazione))
L'attività umana che produce opere di valore estetico.
Human activity that produces works of aesthetic value.
Andrea writes verses, engraves, collects. But art, in him, isn't a vocation: it's furniture. D'Annunzio shows a gifted dilettante who mistakes taste for genius, and his own life for a masterpiece.
artificio · artifice ((sin. espediente, affettazione))
Espediente studiato, che imita il naturale senza esserlo.
A studied device that imitates the natural without being it.
It's Andrea's signature: nothing in him is spontaneous, everything is composed. And artifice repeated for years is no longer a mask you can take off: it has become the face.
asta · auction ((sin. incanto, vendita))
Vendita pubblica in cui un oggetto va a chi offre di più.
A public sale in which an object goes to the highest bidder.
The auctions come back twice and close the circle: at the start Andrea outbids to conquer a woman; at the end another woman's house goes under the hammer. Between them, the whole novel.
azzardo · gambling ((sin. gioco d'azzardo, scommessa))
Il gioco in cui si punta denaro sull'esito del caso.
Play in which money is staked on the outcome of chance.
Gambling is the most honest form of vice in this book: at least here one really loses, and the accounts add up. Manuel Ferres will ruin his wife and daughter over it.
bellezza · beauty ((sin. avvenenza, splendore))
La qualità di ciò che dà piacere allo sguardo o allo spirito.
The quality of what gives pleasure to the eye or the spirit.
In this novel beauty saves no one: it corrupts. It's the only thing Andrea believes in, and it's what lets him justify absolutely anything.
carne · the flesh ((sin. corpo, senso))
Il corpo, considerato nella sua materialità e nei suoi appetiti.
The body, seen in its materiality and its appetites.
Flesh and soul divide the novel like two political parties. Andrea would like to keep them together without choosing: and the book shows that whoever refuses to choose loses both.

Language focus: what you practise

In this level-C1 edition you mainly practise the passato remoto, gerunds and participles, literary vocabulary, shades of register and rhetorical figures. The vocabulary revolves around art, beauty and high society: the key words, with translations, are in the glossary below and recur throughout the text, so you learn them in context.

About the author: Gabriele D'Annunzio

Gabriele D'Annunzio (1863–1938), poet, novelist and legendary figure of Italian life between the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, made the «inimitable life» his banner. Il piacere, written at twenty-five, brought him instant fame.
