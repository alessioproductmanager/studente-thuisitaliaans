La notizia

The News

Giuseppe, il portinaio, trastullava il suo bambino davanti al portone, mostrandogli lo scudo di marmo infisso in cima all’arco — il blasone dei Francalanza, con le sue insegne consumate dal tempo — e la rastrelliera inchiodata al muro del vestibolo dove, ai tempi antichi, i soldati del principe appendevano le armi.1

blasone = coat of arms · viceré = viceroy · stirpe = lineage, stock

1 Il romanzo non comincia da una persona: comincia dallo stemma. Prima ancora dei nomi, De Roberto mostra il simbolo — perché il vero protagonista del libro non è nessuno dei personaggi, ma la casata che li possiede tutti.

The plot of I Viceré

Catania, nineteenth century. On the death of the old princess, the Uzeda di Francalanza family — descendants of the Spanish viceroys of Sicily — hurl themselves into a ferocious war over the inheritance. Ambitious monks, calculated marriages, hatreds handed down from father to son.

As Italy achieves unification, the Uzeda change flags without changing nature: power stays in the family, under any government. A merciless, magnificent novel about the continuity of power, anticipating The Leopard by sixty years.

Why read I Viceré to learn Italian

The family saga keeps your memory in training: names, kinships and alliances intertwine like a nineteenth-century TV series. De Roberto's irony must be caught between the lines — the most valuable reading exercise there is at B2.

About the author: Federico De Roberto

Federico De Roberto (1861–1927), a disciple of Verga and Capuana, carried verismo inside the palaces of the aristocracy. I Viceré, little understood on publication, is now recognised as one of the greatest Italian novels of the nineteenth century.
