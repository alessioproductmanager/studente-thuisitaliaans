CAPITOLO 1

La villa di Cressogno

The Villa at Cressogno

Sul lago di Lugano, in una sera di settembre del 1852, la villa della marchesa Maironi aveva le persiane ancora chiuse. Cressogno, il paese in cui la villa sorgeva, era una piega tra due rive: pochi tetti, un campanile, e barche legate a un muro.1

La marchesa Orsola era arrivata due giorni prima da Brescia. Non l’aveva salutata nessuno, e nessuno se n’era offeso: si sapeva che lei preferiva così.

1 Cressogno esiste davvero: è una frazione di Valsolda, sulla riva orientale del lago. Fogazzaro c’era nato d’estate, e in età adulta ci tornava a scrivere. La geografia del romanzo è, per lui, la geografia di casa.

The plot of Piccolo mondo antico

Valsolda, on Lake Lugano, in the years of Austrian rule. Franco Maironi marries Luisa against the wishes of his grandmother, the harsh Marchioness Orsola, who disinherits him. He is a believer and a dreamer, she is rational and hungry for justice: they love each other, but see the world with opposite eyes.

When sudden tragedy strikes their little daughter, that difference becomes an abyss. Between faith and reason, grief and the Risorgimento, Fogazzaro wrote one of the most intense and moving novels of the Italian nineteenth century.

Why read Piccolo mondo antico to learn Italian

Fogazzaro's prose is lyrical yet controlled: the ideal step before original nineteenth-century texts. The dialogues with dialect touches, explained in the footnotes, are a rare window onto Italian as it was really spoken.

Inside the glossary

Glossary

The key words of the novel, in alphabetical order. Italian definition first, then the English, then what the word means inside this story.

Ceresio · Ceresio (sin. Lago di Lugano, specchio d’acqua)
Il nome antico del Lago di Lugano.
The ancient name of Lake Lugano.
In the novel: In the book the Ceresio is always called simply «the lake»: in the valley the word needs no further specification. Like the word «home» — it is that one, no other.
Risorgimento · Risorgimento (sin. rinascita, unificazione)
Il movimento politico e culturale che, fra il 1815 e il 1861, portò all’unità e all’indipendenza dell’Italia.
The political and cultural movement (1815–1861) that led to Italian unification.
In the novel: The novel is set in the years immediately before the Second War of Independence (1859). Franco is a patriot, the marchesa is pro-Austrian — and the political quarrel is layered on top of the family one.
Valsolda · Valsolda (sin. valle, distretto)
Piccola valle in provincia di Como, sulla riva orientale del Lago di Lugano, ai confini con la Svizzera. È la terra d’origine di Fogazzaro.
Small valley in Como province, on the eastern shore of Lake Lugano, along the Swiss border. Fogazzaro’s own country.
In the novel: The Valsolda in this novel is not a backdrop, it is a character. The villages (Oria, Albogasio, Cressogno, Casarico) have an exact geography, and Fogazzaro counts the distances between them in minutes of rowing. It is a small world, and an old one.
angoscia · anguish (sin. inquietudine, affanno)
Un’inquietudine profonda, con un fondo di paura, per qualcosa che si sente ma non si sa nominare.
A deep unease, tinged with fear, of something one senses but cannot name.
In the novel: For pages and pages before the tragedy, Franco feels an anguish he cannot explain. Fogazzaro will not let him say why — because the reader is not to know yet, either.
austriaco · Austrian (sin. imperiale, asburgico)
Del regno austriaco che, all’epoca del romanzo, occupava il Lombardo-Veneto.
Of the Habsburg empire, which at the time of the novel occupied Lombardy-Venetia.
In the novel: The Valsolda is divided: part borders on free Switzerland, part is Austrian. The Austrian police are there, the censorship too. Fogazzaro rarely shows them — but the air of the book is always heavy with them.
bigotteria · religious hypocrisy (sin. falsa devozione, ipocrisia religiosa)
La religiosità ridotta a forma esteriore, fatta di gesti, di scandalo per le piccole cose e di indifferenza per la carità vera.
Religion reduced to outward gestures, alert to small scandals, blind to real charity.
In the novel: The marchesa and her friends are the very portrait of bigotry: they count the signs of the cross, and never see the hunger of their own kin. Fogazzaro, a Catholic himself, draws them severely.

Language focus: what you practise

In this level-C1 edition you mainly practise the passato remoto, gerunds and participles, literary vocabulary, shades of register and rhetorical figures. The vocabulary revolves around faith, nature and feelings: the key words, with translations, are in the glossary below and recur throughout the text, so you learn them in context.

About the author: Antonio Fogazzaro

Antonio Fogazzaro (1842–1911), from Vicenza, spent his life exploring the conflict between religious faith and modern thought. Piccolo mondo antico, his masterpiece, was born partly of an autobiographical grief.
