CAPITOLO 1

Il giornalino nuovo

The New Diary

Oggi è il mio compleanno. Io ho nove anni.

Io mi chiamo Giannino Stoppani. Io vivo a Firenze, con il babbo, la mamma e le mie tre sorelle: Ada, Luisa e Virginia.

Le mie sorelle sono grandi. Ada ha ventidue anni. Luisa ne ha venti. Virginia diciannove.

Nella mia famiglia tutti dicono che io sono un monello. Le mie sorelle mi chiamano «Gian Burrasca». È il mio soprannome, e mi fa rabbia.

famiglia = family · monello = scamp, imp · guaio = trouble, scrape

1 «Gian Burrasca» vuol dire «Giannino tempesta»: una burrasca è un temporale, e Giannino combina guai come un temporale rompe le cose. È il nome che gli danno le sorelle, e nel libro non si toglierà più: sarà il suo nome per sempre.

The plot of Il giornalino di Gian Burrasca

Giannino Stoppani, known to everyone as Gian Burrasca, is nine years old and has a natural talent for disaster. In his diary he records every prank, every escape and every punishment: stolen fish, experiments gone wrong, hypocritical adults mercilessly exposed.

Sent to boarding school to be «straightened out», Giannino organises his most famous revolution, the one against the rice soup. A classic of Italian humour that makes children laugh — and adults even more.

Why read Il giornalino di Gian Burrasca to learn Italian

Laughing is the best engine for reading on: motivation never runs out here. Giannino's first-person diary is also a perfect model to imitate for writing your own diary in Italian, using everyday vocabulary.

Inside the glossary

Glossary

The key words of the novel, in alphabetical order. Italian definition first, then the English, then what the word means inside this story.

burla · prank, hoax (sin. scherzo, beffa)
Uno scherzo fatto a qualcuno, di solito alle sue spalle.
A joke played on someone, often behind their back.
In the novel: Heavier than a joke: a burla always has someone at the receiving end. Giannino’s begin as small copyings and hidings and grow, one by one, into things that break lives — a fiancé driven out, an election lost.
complotto · plot, conspiracy (sin. cospirazione, trama)
Il mettersi d’accordo di nascosto per fare qualcosa contro qualcuno.
A secret agreement between people to act against someone.
In the novel: At the college Giannino is no longer alone: with Tito, Maurizio and Carlo he becomes part of a band, and every prank is now a plot. It is the first time in the book he has allies — and Vamba is right that four boys against the rector are far more frightening than one boy alone.
disubbidire · to disobey (sin. trasgredire, non ubbidire)
Non fare quello che dicono i genitori o i grandi.
Not to do what the grown-ups say.
In the novel: Giannino disobeys, but almost never out of malice: he disobeys because nobody has ever explained why he ought to obey. It is his only weapon as a child against a world that gives him orders instead of reasons.
divisa · uniform (sin. uniforme, vestito)
Il vestito uguale per tutti quelli che stanno insieme, come i soldati.
The same clothes worn by everyone in a group, as soldiers do.
In the novel: At the college Giannino hates the uniform because it makes him look like everyone else. It is the first time his own clothes have been taken from him, and it is the first piece of himself he loses: a uniform, the book quietly says, wipes a child out — and what is left is a number, in the dormitory, in the refectory, in class.
dote · dowry (sin. corredo, assegno)
I soldi o le cose che una sposa porta con sé nel matrimonio.
The money or goods a family gives to a daughter who marries.
In the novel: In 1907 Italy no woman married without one. The three Stoppani sisters wait for a dowry the way one waits for permission to live, and Aunt Bettina is the possible dowry. Giannino drives the aunt away with a prank — and unknowingly cancels three marriages. It is the book’s first great scrape.
eredità · inheritance (sin. patrimonio, lascito)
I soldi e le cose che restano ai figli quando un parente muore.
The money and things left to the children when a parent dies.
In the novel: The whole Stoppani family lives waiting for Aunt Bettina’s inheritance: to talk about the aunt is to talk about when she will die, and Giannino grasps this as a child. The book says something hard: in Ottocento bourgeois families the death of an old woman was good news, and nobody said so aloud.

Language focus: what you practise

In this level-A1 edition you mainly practise the present tense, the passato prossimo, basic articles and prepositions, short coordinated sentences. The vocabulary revolves around daily life, pranks and school: the key words, with translations, are in the glossary below and recur throughout the text, so you learn them in context.

About the author: Vamba

Vamba, pen name of Luigi Bertelli (1858–1920), was a journalist and founder of the «Giornalino della Domenica», a magazine that revolutionised children's literature in Italy. Gian Burrasca is his immortal creation.
