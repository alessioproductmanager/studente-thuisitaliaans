The alphabet and pronunciation

Italian is read exactly as it is written, with very few exceptions: learn four pronunciation rules and you can read any word. The real work is in c, g and double consonants.

C and G: soft or hard

SoundWhenExamples
c dolce (ci)c + e, icena, cappuccino, ciao
c dura (k)c + a, o, u, hcaffè, cornetto, chianti, bruschetta
g dolce (gi)g + e, igelato, formaggio, Genova
g dura (gh)g + a, o, u, hgatto, ragù, spaghetti, funghi
The letter h is never pronounced: it only hardens c and g (chianti, spaghetti) or distinguishes ho (verb) from o (conjunction).

Three special clusters

ClusterSoundExamples
gncome la n di ...gnocchi, lasagne, bagno
glil morbida, tutta sul palatofamiglia, aglio, bottiglia
sc + e, icome shpesce, prosciutto, uscita

Double consonants change the word

Double consonants are truly heard, longer and stronger: pala and palla are not the same word, nor are caro and carro, sete and sette, pena and penna.

Stress almost always falls on the second-to-last syllable (ra-GAZ-zo). When it falls on the last, it is written: città, caffè, perché.

The most common mistakes

  • un capucino → un cappuccino (the double c is written and heard)
  • i spagetti → gli spaghetti (the h keeps the g hard)
  • la ciesa → la chiesa (c + h + ie: hard sound)

Try it now

Choose the correctly spelled word:

  1. Al bar ordino un e un cornetto.
  2. Stasera mangiamo fatti in casa.
  3. Mi piace il stagionato.
  4. Vorrei una d'acqua.
  5. Il oggi è freschissimo.
