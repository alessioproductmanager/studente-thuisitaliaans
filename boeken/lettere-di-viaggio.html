CAPITOLO 1

Un&#x27;idea rotonda

A Round Idea

Mi chiamo Cristoforo Colombo. Sono nato a Genova, in Italia, nel 1451.

Mio padre lavorava la lana. Ma io, dalla finestra, guardavo solo il porto e le navi.1

A quattordici anni sono andato in mare per la prima volta.

Ho fatto il marinaio per tutta la vita. Ho visto il nord freddo e le coste dell’Africa.

E ho imparato tre cose: a navigare, a leggere le mappe, e a non avere paura dell'oceano.

navigare = to sail, to navigate · mappa = map · oceano = ocean · diario = diary, logbook

1 Colombo è italiano, ma tutte le sue lettere sono in spagnolo: da adulto vive tra Portogallo e Spagna, e scrive nella lingua dei suoi re. Un genovese che scrive in spagnolo di isole americane — la sua vita è già una mappa.

Waar gaat Lettere di viaggio over?

Genua, 1492. Christoffel Columbus steekt een oceaan over die iedereen nog «de Donkere Zee» noemt, met drie kleine schepen en negentig bange mannen. Hij zoekt Azië; hij vindt iets anders — en begrijpt nooit helemaal wat.

Enkele jaren later maakt een Florentijn, Amerigo Vespucci, de oversteek met andere ogen: hij meet, vergelijkt, twijfelt. En hij schrijft de verboden zin: dit is Azië niet, dit is een nieuwe wereld. De echte reizen, naverteld uit hun eigen brieven — de verwondering en de moed, maar ook de ketenen, en de Taino die deze eilanden allang kenden.

Waarom Lettere di viaggio lezen om Italiaans te leren

Een waargebeurd verhaal, echte data, namen die je al kent: je geheugen doet de helft van het begripswerk. De woordenschat van zee en reizen is concreet en nuttig, en de korte hoofdstukken in briefvorm zijn een natuurlijk model voor je eigen eerste brieven in het Italiaans.

In de woordenlijst

Woordenlijst

De kernwoorden van de roman, op alfabet. Eerst de Italiaanse definitie, dan de Engelse, dan wat het woord in dit verhaal betekent.

amaca · hammock (sin. rete da riposo, letto sospeso)
Una rete appesa tra due sostegni, usata per dormire.
A hanging bed made of net or cloth, tied between two points; a Taino word.
In the novel: A Taino word: «hamaca». Europeans had never seen a bed hanging in the air. The sailors copy it at once, and for four centuries every ship in the world will sleep the Taino way.
ammiraglio · admiral (sin. comandante, capo della flotta)
Il capo militare di una flotta.
The highest commander of a fleet of ships.
In the novel: It is the title Columbus demands from the monarchs before sailing, and defends all his life. When they arrest him, he still signs «the Admiral». For him it is not a rank: it is proof that his idea was right.
ammutinamento · mutiny (sin. ribellione, rivolta)
La ribellione dei marinai contro il loro comandante.
A revolt of sailors against their captain.
In the novel: The most feared word on board. In October, after a month without land, the crew stops obeying willingly: there is talk of turning back, perhaps even of throwing the admiral into the sea. Land arrives just in time.
baratto · barter (sin. scambio, commercio senza denaro)
Lo scambio di una cosa con un’altra, senza denaro.
An exchange of things without using money.
In the novel: Glass beads for parrots, little bells for cotton thread: the first contact between the two worlds is barter. It looks like a game between friends. But one side knows the value of the other’s things, and the other does not.
bussola · compass (sin. ago magnetico, strumento di rotta)
Uno strumento con un ago che indica sempre il nord.
An instrument with a needle that always points north.
In the novel: On the ocean, with no land in sight, the compass is the only certainty. That is why the night the needle «goes wrong» is the most dangerous of the voyage: if the compass lies, the road home no longer exists.
canoa · canoe (sin. piroga, imbarcazione)
Una barca stretta e leggera, che si muove con la pagaia.
A light, narrow boat moved with a paddle; a Taino word.
In the novel: A Taino word, brought into Italian through these letters. Taino canoes were carved from a single tree: some carried forty men. In 1503 a canoe will cross the open sea to save Columbus.
