CAPITOLO 1

La festa di Pentecoste

The Feast of Pentecost

Era primavera, e a Parigi c'era una grande festa.

Il re Carlo Magno aveva invitato tutti i cavalieri del mondo.

Erano arrivati cristiani e saraceni, amici e nemici. Per la festa, la guerra si fermava.

Nelle strade c'era musica. Sui palazzi c'erano i colori di cento regni.

Il re ha annunciato un grande torneo: i migliori uomini del mondo, uno contro l'altro, per gioco.1

◆

Alla tavola del re sedevano i paladini: i dodici migliori cavalieri di Francia.

Il primo era Orlando, nipote del re. Nessuna spada poteva ferirlo. La sua si chiamava Durindana, ed era famosa in tutto il mondo.

cavaliere = knight · saraceno = Saracen · guerra = war · torneo = tournament · paladino = paladin · spada = sword

1 Boiardo comincia con una cosa impossibile: cristiani e saraceni che fanno festa insieme, in piena guerra. Non è storia, è fiaba — e te lo dice subito. In questo mondo le regole le fa il racconto.

Waar gaat L'Orlando innamorato over?

Parijs. Koning Karel de Grote heeft alle ridders van de wereld — vriend en vijand — uitgenodigd voor een groot toernooi. Dan komt een meisje binnen dat niemand ooit heeft gezien: ze heet Angelica, komt uit het Oosten, en is de mooiste vrouw ter wereld. Binnen één avond is elke ridder verliefd. Ook Orlando, de sterkste krijger die er is, die nooit van iemand heeft gehouden.

Wat volgt is een achtervolging over de hele wereld: betoverde bossen, magische bronnen die liefde en haat verwisselen, een gouden lans die nooit verliest, reuzen, tovenaars en draken — terwijl vanaf zee legers komen, groter dan welke stad ook. Het gedicht dat Italië leerde avonturen te vertellen, openlijk voortgezet door de Orlando furioso.

Waarom L'Orlando innamorato lezen om Italiaans te leren

Fantasy-avontuur van vóór het bestaan van fantasy: duels, tovenaars en draken houden het tempo hoog, en op A2-niveau is tempo alles. De verhaalstructuren herhalen zich (uitdaging → duel → betovering) en worden een natuurlijke steiger voor je begrip.

In de woordenlijst

Woordenlijst

amore · love ((sin. passione, affetto))
Il sentimento fortissimo che lega una persona a un'altra.
The very strong feeling that binds one person to another.
In the novel: The force that moves the whole book — Boiardo put it in the title. But here love is never chosen: it arrives like a spell, in a single moment, and turns the strongest warrior in the world into a man who cannot sleep. Nobody is safe from it — not kings, not sages.
anello · ring ((sin. cerchietto, vera))
Piccolo cerchio di metallo che si porta al dito.
A small metal circle worn on the finger.
In the novel: Angelica's magic ring: worn, it breaks every enchantment; held in the mouth, it makes her invisible. It is the single most powerful object in the poem — and it belongs to the person everyone is chasing. Ariosto inherited it, ring and all, for the Furioso.
armatura · armour ((sin. corazza, difesa))
Il vestito di ferro che protegge tutto il corpo del guerriero.
The iron clothing that protects the warrior's whole body.
In the novel: The iron suit that makes a knight a fortress — and, once, a monster: Rodamonte scales the walls of Paris in armour made of dragon scales, drinking arrows like rain. Armour in this poem tells you who a man is before he says a word.
assedio · siege ((sin. accerchiamento, blocco))
L'accerchiamento di una città per costringerla ad arrendersi.
Surrounding a city to force it to surrender.
In the novel: The siege is the poem's favourite shape: tents covering the earth to the horizon, a fortress breathing inside. Albracca first, then Paris — same image, sides reversed. Whoever besieges will be besieged: Boiardo builds his symmetries like an architect.
battaglia · battle ((sin. combattimento, scontro))
Scontro armato tra due eserciti.
An armed clash between two armies.
In the novel: The great battle under the walls of Paris gathers every living character onto a single plain — and never ends. Every sword stops in mid-air, forever, because history interrupted the poet. No battle in literature has a stranger fate: it is still being fought.
battesimo · baptism ((sin. sacramento, rito))
Il rito con l'acqua che fa entrare una persona nella fede cristiana.
The rite with water that brings a person into the Christian faith.
In the novel: Agricane, dying, asks his enemy for baptism with the water of the fountain beside them. It is the most solemn page of the poem: two enemies, one dawn, one gesture that turns a duel into something older and larger. Boiardo, who jokes about everything, does not joke here.

Over de auteur: Matteo Maria Boiardo

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1441–1494), graaf van Scandiano en dichter aan het hof van Ferrara, vond met L'Orlando innamorato de grote Italiaanse ridderroman uit. Zijn gedicht breekt midden in een vers af, terwijl «Italië in vlammen opgaat» door de Franse invasie.
