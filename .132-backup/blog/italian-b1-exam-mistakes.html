Seven Mistakes That Fail Italian B1 Candidates

9 July 2026

After years of preparing candidates I keep seeing the same seven mistakes, and almost none of them are about grammar. They are about strategy, and strategy can be fixed in an afternoon once someone points at it. Consider this article that afternoon.

The seven mistakes

One: meeting the format on exam day. The classic and the costliest. The cure is complete, timed simulations until the format is boring; the full argument is in why mock exams matter.

Two: preparing the favourite skill. Readers read more, talkers talk more, and the exam fails them on the section they avoided. The exam passes by thresholds in every section, so your weakest skill sets your result. Diagnose it with a scored simulation and give it the most hours, not the fewest.

Three: treating listening as background noise. Podcasts while cooking do not train exam listening; attended, repeated listening does, ten minutes a day, with the transcript last. The method is in the listening article.

Four: writing without the task. Candidates produce nice Italian that ignores the required points and lose task marks their language deserved. Read the task twice, tick every required element in your draft, then worry about elegance. Details in the writing guide.

Five: cramming instead of spacing. A heroic final weekend feels productive and evaporates by Tuesday; twenty daily minutes across two months build what one weekend cannot. The science is in spaced repetition and twenty minutes a day.

Six: ignoring your own errors. The mistakes you actually make are a personalised syllabus most candidates throw away. Collect them, schedule them, retire them; the system is in error driven learning.

Seven: booking on hope. Booking a session to motivate yourself is expensive motivation if you are two levels away. Measure first, with the honest checks in are you ready for B1, and book when the simulations say so.

The one habit that prevents all seven

You will have noticed the pattern: every mistake is prevented by the same habit, regular complete simulations with honest section scores steering the week.
